# **Sleep in America® Polls**

The Sleep in America® Poll is the National Sleep Foundation’s premier annual review of current sleep topics. The poll was first conducted in 1991\.

## **Sleep in America® Poll 2026**

The 2026 Poll focused on the sleep health of American youth, specifically children ages 0 to 13\. This focus expands on NSF’s previous population surveys related to children and families and reflects NSF’s growing emphasis on family sleep health, recognizing that young children are a central part of the family unit and that their sleep patterns can influence and be influenced by the sleep of parents, siblings, extended family, and caregivers. Key findings showed almost everyone (95%) agrees that good sleep is essential to overall family functioning, but nearly half of parents rarely or never talk with their children about the importance of sleep.

## **Sleep in America® Poll 2025**

National Sleep Foundation’s 2025 Sleep in America® Poll focused on exploring the powerful link between sleep health and an individual’s ability to flourish in life. Flourishing encompasses happiness, productivity at home and work, goal achievement, and fulfilling social relationships—key indicators of well-being. Key findings showed nearly 9 in 10 adults (88%) who report good sleep satisfaction are flourishing, while less than half (47%) of those with poor sleep satisfaction experience the same level of well-being.

## **Sleep in America® Poll 2024**

National Sleep Foundation’s 2024 Sleep in America® Poll found that healthy sleep behaviors are closely linked to mental health in teens. Nearly 80% of teens who earn a grade of ‘B’ or higher for practicing healthy sleep behaviors are also free of significant depressive symptoms.

## **Sleep in America® Poll 2021**

National Sleep Foundation’s 2021 Sleep in America® Poll shows gaps between public sentiment and the effects of clock change. In the National Sleep Foundation’s national, random-sample survey, over 70 percent of those in Daylight Saving Time-observing states said time changes aren’t a problem for them in either direction.

## **Sleep in America® Poll 2020**

The 2020 poll, administered alongside the Sleep Health Index, explored mood and coping with sleepiness. The poll found that Americans feel sleepy on average three times a week with impacts on activities, mood, and acuity.
